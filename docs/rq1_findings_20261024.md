# RQ1 findings: place visibility in the frozen archive

## Question and scope

Which places recur in Chinese-language travel writing, and which absences are visible in the current archive?

This release answers that question only for the current frozen research archive. It is not a census of all Chinese-language travel writing published in mainland China between 1980 and 2025. The archive contains 107 canonically deduplicated texts, 487 text-place relations, and 438 conservatively normalized place entities. The public release contains no full text, long quotation, source URL, or evidence locator.

The 1,091 bibliographic place signals are a separate retrieval layer. They remain `metadata_only` and `uncertain`; they are never added to the text-place relations or interpreted as authorial arrival.

## Finding 1: recurrence is sparse rather than hub-dominated

Of 438 normalized entities, 403 occur in one text only. Only 35 occur in two or more texts. Of those 35 repeated entities, 33 are associated with at least two authors. The strongest recurrent places are 上海市, 东京, and 昆明市, each occurring in four texts written by four authors. 北京市, 北京首都国际机场, 台北市, 新德里, 布拉格, 香港, and 安曼 each occur in three texts.

The current archive therefore does not support a claim that one destination dominates Chinese travel writing. Its stronger result is a long tail of specific encounters, with limited cross-author recurrence. Repetition within one author's work is not treated as broad cultural visibility; the public repeated-place table reports both text and author counts for this reason.

## Finding 2: place visibility is not equivalent to arrival

The frozen relation layer contains 411 `visited`, 62 `mentioned`, 12 `imagined_or_compared`, and 2 `uncertain` relations. A place in a comparison, historical aside, institution, or anticipated itinerary is not silently converted into an authorial footprint. This distinction keeps the archive able to study cultural reference as well as actual arrival.

## Finding 3: current decade profiles describe the archive, not history as a whole

The current denominators are 21 texts in the 1980s, 21 in the 1990s, 20 in the 2000s, 20 in the 2010s, and 25 in the 2020s. The associated relation counts are 129, 69, 86, 80, and 123. These figures identify patterns that merit close reading within the archive. They do not establish population-level growth, decline, or absence because the sample is deliberately constructed and is not a probability sample.

## Map and review boundary

The interactive map displays 161 relations for which the named entity has publishable geometry and the semantic screen recommends retention. Eight relationships are excluded as semantic holds, and line, polygon, ambiguous, or not-yet-publishable entities are not forced into points. Map records retain a pending human spatial-signoff label. The map is therefore a transparent display subset, not the denominator for the findings above.

## What absence means here

An entity absent from the frozen text layer may be absent from the archive because of publication, digitisation, cataloguing, source access, rights, or verification gaps. Q1 can show what this archive currently makes visible. It cannot show that no Chinese travel writer ever wrote about every unrepresented place.

## Remaining work before a stronger Q1 claim

The next audit step is to human-sign the spatial and semantic review queue, then run a sensitivity check that removes or recodes disputed relationships. A later, broader sampling frame is needed before making a four-decade population claim.
